Flit moves files between the devices around you, directly over your Wi-Fi
network.

Open the app and everything on the same network appears: phones, tablets,
laptops, and headless boxes. Tap a device, pick a file, done. No accounts, no
sign-up, no pairing ceremony, and nothing ever leaves your LAN.

* Instant: the transfer engine is running the moment the app opens.
* Local-first: files hop device-to-device, not through any server.
* Secure: every transfer, including discovery and metadata, rides mutual TLS
  with per-device identities and fingerprint pinning.
* Resumable: an interrupted transfer keeps what landed and continues from
  there when the device comes back — no re-sending what you already sent.
* Native: a small Kotlin app built on Android's own networking stack — no
  webviews, no embedded browsers.
* Cross-platform: speaks a fully open, documented protocol (version 2.2), so
  it interoperates with any other compatible client on your network.

Receiving works the way you want: files stream directly into Downloads/Flit,
your own default folder, or the folder you give a trusted device. Received
files open and re-share with a tap. Text notes travel the same wire as files.

Sending works from the app's picker or your system share sheet; if a device is
asleep, your send waits for it and completes when it's back.

The core — discovery, transfers, everything you need to move files — is free
and complete. No ads, no trackers, no analytics.
